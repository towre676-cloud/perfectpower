# Model Counting Competition sources

The dataset is the complete **200-instance Model Counting Competition 2024 Track 1** archive, [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14249068](https://zenodo.org/records/14249068). The organizers' original SHA-1 list was checked for all 200 uncompressed files. `manifest.json` adds archive SHA-256, compressed and original-file SHA-256, exact headers, sizes and original split identities. Even indices were originally public; odd indices were the competition's evaluation set. The benchmark retains every instance, including inputs exceeding our implemented limits. No clauses or variables are removed to make an unsupported case fit.

Three small original public fixtures (028, 030, 118) are bundled for regression and demonstrations, selected by source identity before comparison results. The entire raw corpus is about 1.29 GB and is retrieved exactly by `scripts/fetch_model_counting.py`; the distribution contains its complete manifest rather than embedding the large archive in Git. The manifest is not a substitute for a complete benchmark: the runner checks and runs the entire selected split against the original files.

Reference binaries are pinned in `references.json`. Ganak 2.8.0 runs with `--prob 0`, using deterministic exact integer counting. D4 is the `d4_regular2` binary from the official archived **2024** solver submissions, [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14249109](https://zenodo.org/records/14249109). This is not the 2026 D4 competition entry. The reference-fetch script retrieves the unchanged upstream binaries and verifies identities. They are not distributed or described as PerfectPower implementations. Upstream data and solvers retain their original attribution and licenses; this repository's code license does not assign a new license to them.

The local comparison uses a declared 10-second end-to-end subprocess wall-clock cutoff and a common 6 GiB virtual-address-space limit. It is a latency experiment on competition data, not an official competition submission or ranking. Official 2026 rules and results are at [mccompetition.org](https://mccompetition.org/2026/mc_description), with different time and memory settings. Historical published solve counts cannot be directly compared to our local results.
