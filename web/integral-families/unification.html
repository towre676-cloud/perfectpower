A real Coxeter–Raghava bridge: lattices, moments, and induction

PerfectPower. Mathematical chapter prepared 17 October 2026.

The construction in this chapter replaces an association between pictures by an explicit integral coordinate map and a commuting system of exact operations. The eight-dimensional E₈ Coxeter lattice is identified with an integral cyclotomic companion module, including its bilinear form. Raghava’s inverse-binomial series are then applied to that actual operator. A triangular Bernoulli transformation identifies their traces with the real even Mellin traces of the same operator. Ordinary cyclic induction acts on both descriptions, and the transformation intertwines the two actions. This is the precise sense of unification used here. It does not assert an equivalence of unspecified higher categories or a physical model.

The actual integral lattice map

Let AA be the E₈ Cartan matrix in the simple-root ordering used by e8_periods.py, and let C=s8⋯s1C=s_8\cdots s_1 be its Coxeter matrix, with s1s_1 acting first. In the coefficient basis of

It has an integral two-sided inverse. Thus it is a unimodular change of lattice coordinates, rather than a map available only after allowing rational denominators. Direct multiplication establishes

(Bx)𝖳A(By)=x𝖳Gy,CnB=BDn(n≥0). (Bx)^{\mathsf T}A(By)=x^{\mathsf T}Gy, \qquad C^nB=BD^n\quad(n\ge0). \tag{2}

The norm-two vectors correspond under this map, with their pairings preserved. This supplies an actual cyclotomic realization of the E₈ root lattice and its Coxeter action. It does not identify a list of primitive field-conjugate branches with a list of roots by their common size.

One operator produces the Raghava and Mellin families

Let TT be a finite-order complex linear operator on a finite-dimensional space VV. Its eigenvalues have the form e2πiqe^{2\pi iq}, with representatives 0≤q<10\le q<1. Repeated eigenvalues retain their multiplicities. A finite-order operator is diagonalizable, because its minimal polynomial divides a polynomial XN−1X^N-1 with distinct complex roots. Equivalently, averaging any positive Hermitian inner product over its finite cyclic group makes TT unitary. Thus the following spectral construction does not require the displayed matrix of TT to be Hermitian in its original basis.

The actual adjoint lift and its cancellation

The exact construction in python/perfectpower/e8_lift.py uses the E₈ Lie bracket and simple-root exponentials to construct an actual adjoint Coxeter lift TadT_{\mathrm{ad}}, not merely an abstract 248-dimensional vector space. Its signed root-space cycles have product-one sign around every orbit. Removing those signs by a checked coordinate change gives eight regular cycles of length thirty, together with the Cartan spectrum above. Induction is an exact operation on the moments

Consider a finite weighted phase profile A=∑qwq[q]A=\sum_q w_q[q], with rational weights and 0≤q<10\le q<1. Ordinary cyclic induction of degree d≥1d\ge1 replaces each phase qq by

q+ud,0≤u<d, \frac{q+u}{d},\qquad0\le u<d,

with the original weight on each branch. Write rj(A)r_j(A) for (7) with these weights, including r0(A)=∑qwqr_0(A)=\sum_qw_q.

Moment transfer theorem. For every j≥0j\ge0,

rj(InddA)=∑k=0jMd(j,k)rk(A), r_j(\operatorname{Ind}_d A)=\sum_{k=0}^j M_d(j,k)r_k(A), \tag{18}

where

Md(j,k)=4j−kd−2j(2j−2k)!∑u=0d−1(u−d−12)2j−2k. M_d(j,k)=\frac{4^{j-k}d^{-2j}}{(2j-2k)!} \sum_{u=0}^{d-1}\left(u-\frac{d-1}{2}\right)^{2j-2k}. \tag{19}

The diagonal is Md(j,j)=d1−2jM_d(j,j)=d^{1-2j}, including Md(0,0)=dM_d(0,0)=d. To prove the formula, write the centered phase of an induced branch as

q+ud−12=q−1/2+u−(d−1)/2d. \frac{q+u}{d}-\frac12 =\frac{q-1/2+u-(d-1)/2}{d}.

Expand its even power by the binomial theorem. Every odd power of the second centered term sums to zero under u↔d−1−uu\leftrightarrow d-1-u. The remaining even terms give (18)–(19), with the factorials cancelling exactly as displayed. The finite centered sums can be evaluated by the Bernoulli–Faulhaber formula, so the algorithm does not have to enumerate an enormous number of branches.

Inducing first by ee and then by dd produces the same phases, with multiplicity, as induction by dede: the two branch indices combine by the ordinary mixed-radix bijection. Therefore

MdMe=Mde. M_dM_e=M_{de}. \tag{20}

The finite matrices here are truncated at a common order; triangularity makes the same equality valid at every such truncation. The full-fiber root-of-unity filter also gives

zk(InddA)=d1−2kzk(A),k≥1. z_k(\operatorname{Ind}_d A)=d^{1-2k}z_k(A),\quad k\ge1.

Together with mass scaling, this proves the commuting identity

ℬKMd=diag(d,d−1,d−3,…,d1−2K)ℬK. \boxed{\mathcal B_K M_d =\operatorname{diag}(d,d^{-1},d^{-3},\ldots,d^{1-2K})\mathcal B_K.} \tag{21}

It is also an independent consequence of the Bernoulli multiplication formula. Equation (21) is the exact connection among branch filtering, Raghava moments and Mellin traces. It makes no appeal to a numerical resemblance between their values.

Why the tempting 240-to-240 identification fails

The primitive branches above the fixed phase ζ1680\zeta_{1680} have exponents

u=1+1680t,0≤t<264,gcd(u,443520)=1. u=1+1680t,\quad0\le t<264,\quad\gcd(u,443520)=1.

There are 240 such branches. Every exponent satisfies u≡1(mod30)u\equiv1\pmod{30}. Restricting their characters from C443520C_{443520} to its natural C30C_{30} subgroup therefore gives 240 copies of the single phase ζ30\zeta_{30}. The root-space part of the actual E₈ Coxeter lift gives eight copies of every thirtieth-root phase. The multiplicity functions disagree, so no equivariant isomorphism exists for this proposed natural restriction. An arbitrary bijection of the two underlying sets cannot repair that representation-theoretic obstruction.

The integral lattice isomorphism (1) and the commuting formulas (9), (18) and (21) avoid this problem. They identify specified operators and preserve specified forms or transformations. They do not require the false fixed-fiber identification.
