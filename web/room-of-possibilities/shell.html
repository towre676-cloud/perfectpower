What this demonstrates

A useful answer can become a reusable collection.

The workshop evaluates a deliberately small, finite design space. Its spatial map reveals which objects survive the requirements. Counting, selecting, and comparing all use that same collection.

The equation exhibit shows the stronger mathematical idea: a complete structured family can support exact counts and addresses even when its population is far too large to enumerate.

Definitions, sources, and a text view

The workshop domain is motor 1–12, gears 1–8, arm 1–3. Lifting score is floor(motor × gears / arm). Size score is motor + gears + arm. Cost score is 8 × motor + 3 × gears + 4 × arm.

The equation exhibit uses all integer points of 2x² = 3y³ with y ≤ 6T². Points are ordered by the source's charts: zero, positive x, negative x; increasing t within each nonzero chart. The illustrated 17 points use T = 8. Coordinates are arranged in a parameter chart, not drawn at their actual enormous spatial scale.

Inside the exact family

A whole room in three pieces.

The origin has one point. The positive branch has T points. The negative branch has T points. Together they have 1 + T + T = 2T + 1 points, with every integer solution counted once.

The selected point, worked out

2(±18t³)² = 648t⁶ = 3(6t²)³

Squaring x removes its sign. Cubing y gives the same number on the other side. The origin satisfies the equation too.

Why this covers every integer solution

If y is negative, its cube is negative while 2x² is nonnegative, so the equation cannot hold. If either coordinate is zero, both must be zero. For a nonzero solution, think of each number as a product of prime building blocks. The equation makes the counts of every prime agree on the two sides.

A square doubles each prime's exponent; a cube triples it. For primes other than 2 and 3, this forces the exponents in x and y to be 3k and 2k. For prime 2, the extra 2 on the left forces exponents 1 + 3k and 1 + 2k. For prime 3, the extra 3 on the right forces exponents 2 + 3k and 1 + 2k. Each k is a nonnegative integer.

Collect those prime building blocks into a positive integer t. Then |x| = 2 × 3² × t³ = 18t³, and y = 2 × 3 × t² = 6t². Both signs of x work. The origin and these two branches therefore cover all integer points. The ceiling T restricts the family to y ≤ 6T².
