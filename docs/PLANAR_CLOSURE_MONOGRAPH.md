# Real source geometry and analytic metric closure

This layer closes the rational-to-real interpretation gap and certifies pointwise coverage for arbitrary supplied meshes within an explicit region semantics. It does not prove a Jordan theorem or global continuous-path completeness. The finite obstacle objective remains Manhattan distance. Physical rope dynamics and positive clearance remain outside the result.

## Universal certificates

`PlanarCertificates.lean` defines affine rational halfplanes evaluated at actual real points, a Boolean formula language, and finite three-way sign trees. Splits cover negative, zero and positive signs; thus tangencies, shared boundaries and lower-dimensional strata participate in the proof. A leaf must bind every formula atom and evaluate the actual source formula. An impossible leaf supplies a sparse rational nonnegative combination of constraints whose variable coefficients vanish and whose constant is negative, or whose constant is zero with a positive-weight strict constraint. `contradiction_sound` proves that no real point satisfies such a combination. `checked_sound` and `universal_identity` interpret a checked tree at every real point. These are generic theorems, not enumerated geometry examples.

Python proposes these witnesses through exact two-dimensional Fourier–Motzkin elimination. Lean checks constraint membership, all weights and strictness, the rational coefficient identity, source formula binding, and the complete split structure. No linear-programming oracle, floating-point tolerance or sampled point is trusted. The producer rejects feasible counterexample sign regions. Work and atom limits reject the request rather than truncate its proof.

The emitter generates individual checked leaf proofs and composes internal nodes with the generic `checked_split` theorem. This avoids repeatedly normalizing a large complete proof tree in a single decision tactic. The retained concave mesh has 2,743 nodes and 1,740 contradiction leaves; its interpretation still follows the same universal theorem.

## Real regions, segments and boundary contact

`PlanarGeometry.lean` gives real determinant and closed-segment predicates, source polygon edges, and a signed half-open ray count. The filled source region is its boundary union the points of nonzero winding; its forbidden interior is nonzero winding with the boundary removed. Source vertices and coefficients are rational, but the query point is real. Formula specification theorems prove this interpretation, including horizontal edges and endpoints. This definition is explicit for all finite source chains. The route producer additionally admits simple polygons; the mesh producer additionally checks simplicity, positive triangle orientation, exact separation, atomic boundary cancellation and area identities. Those producer checks are not silently promoted into a Lean Jordan theorem.

`RealVisibility.segment_from_tree` proves that every real parameter between zero and one avoids every supplied winding interior. A blocked certificate carries an actual rational parameter and uses `blocked_segment` to refute that universal real predicate. Boundary travel and isolated tangencies are permitted. A zero-length segment is admitted exactly when its stationary point avoids the forbidden interiors.

`RealContact.lean` proves existence or nonexistence of a real common point of two actual closed segments. Contact admission checks a supplied rational common point and casts its exact formula interpretation to the real plane. Disjointness uses the universal sign tree. Proper-crossing, touching and overlap labels remain producer metadata; the emitted Lean statement proves contact or disjointness, rather than a full classification theorem. A polygon-boundary query decomposes into its finite source edges.

## Complete finite obstacle graph and all-walk bounds

The real route emitter checks each distinct ordered source-vertex pair, including every blocked pair. `real_complete` therefore characterizes the retained admitted graph exactly by whole-real-parameter segment visibility. Its sources include the actual original polygon lists and vertex lists; missing and invented edges cannot be repaired by a certificate for a different graph. Source-bound reconstruction and weights from the preceding rational layer are retained.

`real_walk_to_graph` transports every real-visible scaled source walk to the reconstructed weighted graph. `real_optimal_walk` then applies the existing generic `GeometryCertificates.optimal_walk` theorem. The emitter independently certifies that the stationary source segment avoids forbidden interiors, including zero-step routes. It also constructs an explicit `retained_real_walk` from the actual accepted path and independently checks its original-unit cost. The original-unit `SourceWalk` additionally includes stationary steps and repeated vertices. Its feasible rational potential proves `source_potential_bound` and `source_optimal_walk` for every finite source walk, with original rational Manhattan cost. Closed reachable sets give `source_no_walk_outside`; an unreachable route emitter uses the same complete real admission equivalence to rule out every such walk.

This is proven geometric completeness of the finite source-vertex graph for the explicitly defined segment predicate. It is not a proof that every arbitrary continuous path can be replaced by a source-vertex walk of no greater length. No optimality claim is made for unrestricted bending paths, positive-clearance routes, Euclidean length or physical dynamics.

## Mesh coverage

`coverage_from_tree` proves, for every real point, equivalence between the closed nonzero-winding source region and the union of the supplied closed counterclockwise triangles. This supports concave polygons and interior Steiner vertices without a particular refinement trace. It verifies coverage directly, independently of Python replay and independently of an inference from area and atomic cancellation. The accepted mesh must pass both the existing finite mesh admission and this new universal coverage checker. The old `mesh-proof` interface still states only its older arithmetic guarantees; `real-mesh-proof` is the strengthened admission.

A proof deriving arbitrary mesh coverage solely from simple-polygon topology, separation and boundary cancellation remains open. So does a general equivalence between the chosen winding region and an independently developed Jordan interior. The present universal certificate establishes the desired pointwise coverage for each admitted instance without assuming either missing theorem.

## Analytic consequences of the Gudmundsson extraction

`FlatRiemannian.lean` proves the actual derivative of an affine real line, its smoothness and zero acceleration, and uniqueness of any everywhere differentiable real curve with differentiable velocity and everywhere zero acceleration from its initial position and velocity. For a constant positive metric it identifies each timed segment's action divided by two with its actual interval integral. The sum of these integrals equals the finite path energy, and the generic sharp energy lower bound applies to every finite positively timed sequence. This is a sum of segment integrals; a theorem for a single concatenated curve and its almost-everywhere derivative has not been asserted.

`MetricJetRealization.lean` realizes any positive planar symmetric metric value and symmetric first derivatives as an affine smooth metric field. It proves the value and actual coordinate derivatives at the source origin and positivity throughout some neighborhood. The affine field need not remain positive globally. `MetricJetConnection.lean` derives raised connection coefficients by the exact inverse metric, identifies the input derivatives with actual field derivatives at the origin, proves torsion symmetry and compatibility, and proves smoothness of the coefficients at every point where the field is positive. The generic lowered Koszul uniqueness theorem remains available. This closes local first-jet realization and its connection interpretation; it is not a manifold atlas theorem, a global Levi-Civita construction, curvature formalization or nonflat geodesic ODE existence theorem.

## Reproduction and receipts

`make planar-closure` regenerates the mesh, segment, contact and route examples and runs the new adversarial Python suite. `make planar-closure-kernel` compiles the generic modules and every retained exact source and demands mathematical rejection of deliberately false claims. CI runs this audit separately from the earlier certificate and metric audits. The CLI operations are `real-mesh-proof`, `real-segment-proof`, `real-contact-proof` and `real-route-proof`; each returns `certificate` and `lean_source`.

Emission always records `kernel_checked: false`. `receipts/planar_closure/kernel-validation.json` separately pins the accepted source hashes and the rejected corruption hashes. `python-validation.json` records the adjacent regression campaign. The kernel audit permits only the standard printed axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`; it rejects `sorryAx`, custom axioms and native decision shortcuts. Missing imports, compiler failures and exhausted resources do not count as mathematical rejection. Optional `--extend` preserves previously accepted, byte-identical emitted sources while freshly checking the generic modules and newly added sources; its receipt identifies those reused hashes. The default CI invocation replays everything.

The retained campaign passes 116 Python regression tests, including 19 new planar test methods. The kernel receipts cover 11 generic modules and 16 emitted sources, with nine deliberate mathematical rejections. The examples include concave and Steiner meshes, tangent and overlapping boundaries, real contact and disjointness, rational-unit routes, and an independently admitted zero-length route. The Python disconnected-region adversary supplements the generic Lean closed-reachability theorem; it is not presented as a retained real disconnected-graph Lean instance.
