## Reflection dynamics and rational positive geometry

For four signed integer curvatures satisfying (a+b+c+d)²=2(a²+b²+c²+d²), replacing a by 2(b+c+d)−a preserves the equation. Applying the same replacement twice returns a. Lean proves both claims directly, with no hypothesis about whether the four circles are realized in a particular packing. Keeping two curvatures fixed, with sum s, produces the alternating recurrence b_(n+2)=2b_(n+1)−b_n+2s. The recursively defined sequence agrees for every natural n with u+(v−u−s)n+sn². In the principal fixture, u=2, v=3, s=1, this becomes n²+2, so the fifth term is the exact cube 27. The theorem establishes the orbit formula for all indices. It does not turn the earlier bounded cube scan into a complete perfect-cube classification.

The interval canonical coefficient (b−a)/((x−a)(b−x)) satisfies the subdivision identity through an intermediate point c. Every denominator exclusion appears in the Lean statement. The collision chart x=tu, y=t has Jacobian t in the chosen wedge order du∧dt, and the corresponding rational coefficient becomes 1/(u(1−u)t(1−t)). This is a proved chart identity. Stable compactification, boundary orientation conventions beyond this chart, and intrinsic conformal geometry are not silently inferred from it.

The theta connection example is now represented by an actual two-by-two complex matrix. Its diagonal entries are a+b+c; its off-diagonal entries are −a+b−ci and −a+b+ci. The determinant is exactly Q=4ab+2ac+2bc. The imaginary contributions are essential: replacing this matrix by an ordinary real matrix would incorrectly retain an additional c² term. The rational Jacobian numerator for the supplied marginal coordinates is 16abc²Q. Dividing by the marginal denominator product gives 1/(ab), under explicit nonzero hypotheses. These statements certify the algebra used by the exact Python packet. The numerator identity itself is not a general theorem constructing a differentiable positive-geometry map.

The quotient metric argument is similarly precise. Given three corners per triangle and two triangle incidences per edge, the total defect divided by π is 2V−corners/3=2(V−E+F). Lean checks all eight saved metric packets. Six saved associahedral face vectors satisfy the closed-polytope alternating sum one, and their boundary vectors have the recorded alternating sums. The finite certificates do not prove a general theorem identifying the entire moduli-space compactification with an associahedron.

## A determinant theorem beyond the fixture

ConnectionDeterminant proves two-column weighted Cauchy–Binet for an arbitrary finite edge type. Let B have two complex columns, let w be arbitrary complex weights, and put G_ab=Σ_i w_i conjugate(B_ia)B_ib. With M_ij=B_i0 B_j1−B_j0 B_i1, the theorem states

2 det G = Σ_i Σ_j w_i w_j conjugate(M_ij) M_ij.

The ordered-pair convention counts every unordered basis twice. Repeated indices contribute zero, and no nonzero-weight or connected-support assumption is needed. The proof expands each squared minor and factors the finite sums, then applies the actual matrix determinant formula. Thus it covers every two-vertex connection packet in the current setting, rather than just one saved numerical graph. Positivity requires real nonnegative weights in the chosen embedding; arbitrary complex weights do not inherit that order property. The general arbitrary-column Cauchy–Binet theorem, full gain-graph support-rank argument, and matroid greedy optimality remain structural targets. The two-column theorem is named accordingly.

## Differential regularity arithmetic

The differential constructor uses floor(je/n) to cancel the apparent pole at a branch root of reduced multiplicity e. The formal proof begins by showing that gcd(n,e) divides (je mod n). Since that remainder is strictly below n, the positive gap n−(je mod n) is at least gcd(n,e). Clearing the local ramification denominator gives je+gcd(n,e)≤n floor(je/n)+n. This is exactly the integer inequality behind nonnegative finite order.

At infinity, Lean proves the degree cutoff equivalence n(A+i)+n+δ≤jM if and only if i+1≤floor((jM−nA−δ)/n), with n positive. The truncated natural subtraction is handled explicitly, including empty character spaces. Multiplying by x^i adds a nonnegative finite order under the supplied valuation signs. These proofs establish the constructor's arithmetic conditions. They do not themselves construct local parameters on a normalized algebraic curve or establish the completeness of a holomorphic differential basis.

## Formal Legendre equations and actual series bounds

LegendreBounds defines a_0=1 and a_(n+1)=a_n(2n+1)²/(4(n+1)²). Lean proves that every coefficient is positive, the sequence is decreasing, and (n+1)²a_(n+1)=(n+1/2)²a_n. The formal power series built from these coefficients satisfies the Picard–Fuchs equation X(1−X)F″+(1−2X)F′−F/4=0. This is an actual identity in Mathlib's formal power series ring, proved by equality of every coefficient, not a tested finite prefix.

The analytic part completed here concerns the real series itself. For any nonnegative decreasing sequence a and real 0≤λ<1, Lean proves summability of Σ a_n λ^n and the geometric tail bound

0 ≤ Σ a_n λ^n − Σ_(n<N) a_n λ^n ≤ a_N λ^N/(1−λ).

The proof dominates the original sequence by a_0 times a geometric series, then applies the stronger a_N bound to the shifted tail. Instantiating the result with the Legendre recurrence yields a rigorous lower and upper interval for its real sum at every such λ and every N. This removes the unproved elementary tail argument from the earlier Python construction. The Euler-integral theorem identifying the series with normalized elliptic periods still needs formalization, as does the corresponding analytic cycle orientation. No approximate integration or assumed period identity occurs in the series theorem.

## Recovered multiplicative equations

The August system T⁶K³=P⁴, K¹⁸τ=R⁹, E⁶⁰=τ⁵g²⁴, a⁵=ζE² has two historical elimination consequences. Lean proves T³⁶R⁹=P²⁴τ and a¹⁵⁰=ζ³⁰τ⁵g²⁴ directly over the rationals, even at zero, because these two arguments do not cancel factors. For the middle equations with nonzero denominators, it proves that c₃/c₂ is the cube of E²⁰R³/(τ²g⁸K⁶).

The strongest new compatibility statement works in integer exponent coordinates. The entire four-row exponent system has a solution in nine integers if and only if 3 divides b₃−b₂. Necessity follows from the explicit row combination. Sufficiency supplies all nine coordinates constructively from a witness for that divisibility. This confirms the single modulus-three image obstruction without relying on an externally computed Smith transcript. It does not by itself formalize reconstruction of every positive rational point from all prime valuations, nor the general Python solver's execution. The concrete positive-integer relation 27x²=4y³ together with xy=6 and x,y positive is also proved complete: exactly x=2,y=3.

## Making the unfinished quartic checks feasible

The remaining quartics exposed a practical defect in the older proof computation. Scaling a monic quartic to an integer near-square expression inflated the constant residual and therefore the conservative coordinate bound. A representative input L=1,u=v=w=1,z=−199 normalized to a bound of 12,793, requiring 25,587 candidate x coordinates before the kernel could check the list. The large reduction exhausted the available memory, even though the resulting point set contained only (7,±51).

QuarticCutoff proves a sharper criterion. For q(x)=Lx²+ax+b and residual r(x)=cx+d, every solution outside the zero-residual fibre satisfies |L||x|²≤(|a|+|c|)|x|+|b|+|d|. A supplied nonnegative B satisfying |L|B≥|a|+|c| and |L|B²>(|a|+|c|)B+|b|+|d| therefore contains every such solution. The monotonicity proof is explicit integer algebra. The native command searches for B, and the emitted Lean theorem independently checks both inequalities.

The exceptional fibre is retained rather than discarded. If c is nonzero and cx+d=0 has an integer root, that root is added to the finite coordinate set even when it lies outside [−B,B]. The set is filtered and its square roots are checked using the same exact routines as before. A regression includes x=100,y=±10000 for y²=x⁴+x−100, outside its domination interval. The difficult earlier example now uses B=43, or 87 interval coordinates, and its complete list checks in 3.5 seconds. The original catalogue equation source and expected point lists stay unchanged; the stronger proof-producing command makes their existing obligations practical to discharge.
