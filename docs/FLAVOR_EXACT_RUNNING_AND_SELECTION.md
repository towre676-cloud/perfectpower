## An exact finite-mass exclusion for a pure adjoint source

The simplest source-label reduction removes the two singlets from each nonet. Its complete minimal scalar action has 15 coefficients, including 12 quartics and the Higgs; the corresponding nonconstant running counts are 24 without, or 33 with, the Gaussian finite mediators. This simplification cannot supply a strong hierarchy under universal canonical fermion matching.

Let D have blocks (a I,0; C,M I), with a,M positive and C traceless Hermitian. For nondegenerate light singular values 0 < m1 < m2 < m3 < min(a,M), each squared source eigenvalue is f(m) = (a squared - m squared)(M squared - m squared)/m squared. The largest source magnitude belongs to m1. Tracelessness in three dimensions forces that magnitude to equal the sum of the other two magnitudes, including either possible overall sign. The following two exact positive differences establish the bound without a large-M approximation.

f(m1) - (m2/m1) squared f(m2) = (m2 squared - m1 squared)(a squared + M squared - m1 squared - m2 squared)/m1 squared > 0. Likewise, (m2/m3) squared f(m2) - f(m3) = (m3 squared - m2 squared)(a squared + M squared - m2 squared - m3 squared)/m3 squared > 0. Taking positive square roots and imposing the trace condition gives m2/m1 < 1 + m2/m3 < 2.

Consequently this canonical universal pure-adjoint candidate cannot reproduce strongly separated first and second quark masses. Degenerate or zero source eigenvalues are boundary cases; they do not evade the nondegenerate hierarchy obstruction. Singlet shifts, nonuniversal heavy or bare Higgs matrices, or nontrivial quark metrics change the assumptions. They are possible mechanisms requiring their own justified contractions, rather than exceptions to this theorem.
