## Arbitrary-dimensional Cauchy–Binet

For finite index sets n and e and matrices M of shape n×e and N of shape e×n, Lean proves

```text
(card n)! det(MN) = sum over p:n→e of det(M[:,p]) det(N[p,:]).
```

The proof expands the second minor over permutations, interchanges finite sums and reindexes selections by each permutation. The sign from permuting the selected columns matches the sign in the determinant expansion. Each resulting inner sum is the rectangular determinant expansion proved in the preceding push. The factorial is exactly the number of permutations. This argument works in any commutative ring, including positive characteristic, because it never divides by the factorial. Repeated selections contribute zero through repeated columns or rows, so degeneracies and zero weights need no exclusions.

The weighted Gram specialization takes an arbitrary e×n matrix B and weights w in a commutative star ring. Its Gram entries are sums of star(B_ia) w_i B_ib, and Lean proves

```text
(card n)! det Gram(B,w)
  = sum over p:n→e of (product over i of w_(p i)) star(det B_p) det B_p.
```

Conjugate transpose gives the starred determinant and row scaling gives the weight product. This is the full general weighted squared-minor identity with ordered selections, removing the old two-column limitation. In a characteristic-zero ring without zero divisors, vanishing of every maximal minor now proves the weighted Gram determinant zero by cancelling the nonzero factorial. No positivity or connected-support hypothesis is smuggled into this implication. A separate unordered-subset formulation would remove the explicit multiplicity; general gain-graph rank, matroid greedy correctness and implementation refinement of the algebraic-bit sampler still need their own proofs.

## Variable-width factorial arithmetic

`FactorialWindow` proves the p-adic valuation of N!/(N−W)! as a difference of factorial valuations and then as two finite Legendre sums. At the native level, natural subtraction makes W>N a truncated window; the public Python operation requires 0≤W≤N. A valuation not divisible by k proves that the positive window product is not a k-th power. The Python witness search uses the bit length of N and a bounded supplied prime list, so it can process widths that cannot be enumerated. Passing every tested prime produces UNKNOWN.

The retained generated theorem proves that (10³⁰)!/(10³⁰−10²⁰)! is not a square. Its 2-adic valuation is 100000000000000000011, which is odd. Lean evaluates the finite floor sums and checks logarithmic truncation bounds through a power inequality; it does not reduce either enormous factorial. This closes a useful variable-width Gamma-ratio obstruction route. ## Certified periods, inverses and marked monodromy
