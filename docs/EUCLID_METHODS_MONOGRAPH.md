# Euclidean arithmetic and exact geometry in PerfectPower

**The concrete result.** PerfectPower now has a resultant-controlled compiler for product power equations, rational Euclidean contact geometry and bounded Voronoi cells, an exact quadratic-surd field interface, continued-fraction Pell production, Euclidean gcd transcripts, and certified exhaustion of square roots and isolated polynomial roots. These operations are available from the existing package and the integrated `euclid` command. They connect to the graph populations and string winding machinery added in the preceding release. This work was motivated by Euclid's *Elements*, Richard Fitzpatrick's Greek–English edition: https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/Books/Euclid/Elements.pdf. The implementation uses modern polynomial resultants, prime valuations, coordinate geometry, continued fractions and Sturm theory. Those modern ingredients are not attributed to Euclid.

**The arithmetic reduction.** Supply nonconstant integer polynomials A and B, with coefficients listed from constant term upward, and an exponent d between two and sixty-four. The compiler forms a Sylvester matrix, computes its determinant by fraction-free Bareiss elimination, and recovers integer cofactors U and V satisfying UA+VB=R, where R is the nonzero resultant. Coefficient arithmetic independently checks that identity. Evaluating at any integer n shows that every common divisor of A(n) and B(n) divides R. The compiler rejects a zero resultant because common polynomial factors require a different reduction. It supports degrees up to twelve per factor, checks coefficient and determinant growth, and refuses an over-budget computation rather than silently dropping cases.

**Why finitely many branches suffice.** Let P be the primes dividing R. For nonzero A(n) and B(n), a prime outside P cannot divide both values. If their product is a d-th power, the exponent of such a prime in whichever value it divides must therefore be a multiple of d. At each exceptional prime p, the residual exponents e and f satisfy e+f=0 modulo d. The compiler enumerates e from zero through d−1 and chooses f as its complementary residue. It builds coefficients cA and cB from those primes and emits the equations A(n)=cA u^d and B(n)=cB v^d. For even d, both factors must have the same sign, and the compiler includes both positive and negative coefficient pairs. For odd d, signed roots retain the factor signs. Integer zeros of either polynomial form a separate exact branch. This is a complete reduction of the original equation; it does not solve every resulting simultaneous power equation.

**A reusable explanation.** The resultant certificate carries the original polynomial lists, cofactors and obstruction constant. The public checker verifies the source binding and polynomial identity without rerunning determinant discovery. Any nonzero integral Bezout constant is a sound obstruction, even when it is not the smallest possible one. The canonical descent checker additionally regenerates the source-bound branch packet. An input classifier recovers the factor roots and corresponding branch without factoring the evaluated values. A gcd receipt shows the evaluated identity and actual common divisor. The finite population adapter connects this classification to `GraphPopulation` over a declared integer interval, exposing exact count, rank and selection. That adapter concerns the interval only and does not establish a new global enumeration theorem.

**Formal acceptance for all inputs.** The Lean foundation defines polynomial coefficient addition, scaling, multiplication and Horner evaluation. It proves that evaluation respects those operations. The source-bound emitter then asks the kernel to check the literal coefficient identity and transports it through those theorems. The result is a theorem that UA+VB=R at every integer input and that every common integer divisor divides R. The retained fixtures include both a linear product and a quadratic–linear product. The kernel accepts the correct sources and rejects an altered coefficient identity. This formally establishes the gcd restriction for each accepted emitted source. The complete prime-factor branch compiler, trial factorization, JSON parser and general Python execution are separately tested and replayed, rather than established by this Lean route.

**Exact contact and distance.** The geometry interface accepts integers, rational strings and `Fraction` objects, and rejects floating-point coordinates. Orientation is an exact determinant. Segment intersection distinguishes proper crossing, endpoint contact, collinear overlap and disjointness, including degenerate point segments. Point-to-segment distance is returned as a rational squared distance with its exact nearest point and clamped projection parameter. Segment–disk contact compares that squared distance against the squared rational radius. No square root is needed for these decisions. Disk contact is intersection with the closed disk; the tangent flag records equality of the minimum distance and radius.

**Voronoi cells and ties.** Comparing squared distances to sites a and b gives the halfplane 2x·(b−a)≤||b||²−||a||². The implementation clips a declared rational rectangle by all such halfplanes for each site. It returns exact rational vertices and the defining inequalities, preserving coincident-site ties and lower-dimensional cells. Nearest-site queries retain every tied owner. Random tests verify that every returned cell vertex actually has its declared site among the nearest owners. The result is Euclidean geometry on the supplied plane and rectangle. It does not claim to compute intrinsic nearest-site regions on a curved surface or change the earlier analytic geometry contract.

**String integration.** The combined string interface uses the preceding release's winding code together with the new disk-contact predicates. For a closed polygon it reports each object's winding and whether any string segment intersects its disk. A disk is marked wholly topologically enclosed only when its center has nonzero winding and no segment contacts the disk. A center on a string segment has undefined winding; an open string is not assigned a polygon winding. These distinctions prevent enclosure from being confused with contact. Physical friction, thickness, tension, grasp stability and motor control are not inferred. This release supplies the mathematical interface inside PerfectPower; it does not rebuild the Windows STRING executable or modify another repository.

**Quadratic irrational objects.** `QuadraticSurd` represents a+b√D with rational a and b and a positive nonsquare integer D. Addition, subtraction, multiplication and inversion stay in that exact field. The norm a²−Db² controls inversion. Opposite-sign terms are compared by their squared magnitudes, with signs retained; floors use exact comparisons inside rational bounds. Mixing different quadratic fields is refused rather than approximated. Square radicands are excluded from this interface because rational values have a simpler representation. The interface deliberately covers quadratic fields rather than arbitrary nested radicals or general algebraic numbers.

**Euclidean algorithms and Pell.** The gcd route returns division steps and signed Bezout coefficients, including zero and negative inputs. Its checker verifies both the transcript and arithmetic certificate. The continued-fraction route records the complete-quotient recurrence for √D, its period and the repeated state. A separate checker replays the integer recurrence and checks each quotient floor using exact quadratic-surd comparisons. Convergents are then formed until the first positive norm-one Pell unit appears. The retained D=61 example is 1766319049+226153980√61, and Lean checks its norm directly. The producer agrees with the existing Pell solver for every nonsquare D from two through one hundred. General continued-fraction fundamentality is not newly kernel-certified by this receipt; the existing family certificate route keeps its own stronger bounds and acceptance requirements.

**Exhaustion with an explicit tolerance.** The square-root route starts with rational enclosing endpoints, bisects, and records the exact comparison between each midpoint square and the radicand. It returns when the rational width reaches the requested tolerance or the root is found exactly. The packet also records the dyadic width bound and is replayable. A source-bound Lean emitter checks the final endpoint-square inequalities, nonnegative endpoints and tolerance by clearing rational denominators. The polynomial route first removes repeated factors, forms an exact Sturm chain and requires an initial interval containing exactly one distinct root with root-free endpoints. It bisects using exact root counts, so even-multiplicity roots need no sign change. The final enclosure and halving transcript are replayed in Python; this release does not claim a general Lean proof of the Sturm producer.

**Evidence and practical limits.** Fourteen focused test methods cover one hundred randomized polynomial-pair attempts, signed branch classification over two hundred and one integer inputs for each retained factor/exponent combination, finite population access, random Voronoi vertex checks, segment degeneracies and symmetry, string integration, quadratic field operations, ninety nonsquare Pell inputs, nine hundred and sixty-one signed gcd pairs, rational square-root bounds, repeated-root isolation and corrupted packets. A precision stress case at coordinate scale 2^60 has exact orientation determinant one while the corresponding binary64 expression evaluates to zero. Two sites separated by one also collapse to the same binary64 coordinate there, while the rational nearest-site query chooses the correct owner. These are reproducible correctness differences, not a runtime benchmark or claim of industrial solver superiority. Factorization, exceptional-prime branching, large Sturm computations and dense Voronoi clipping have explicit scope and work limitations.

**Reproduction and use.** `make euclid-methods` runs the tests and regenerates the worked packets. `make euclid-methods-kernel` checks the foundation, the two source-bound polynomial identities and the rational square-root bounds using Lean 4.20. Set `PERFECTPOWER_LEAN` if the executable has a different location. Run `python -m perfectpower euclid descent examples/euclid_methods/descent.json`, or choose `voronoi`, `contacts`, `pell`, `sqrt-bounds` or `root-bounds` with the corresponding example. The `string` operation accepts the contacts example as well and returns the combined contact/enclosure report. The complete source archive includes the examples, generated proof sources, receipts, rejection evidence and this monograph.
