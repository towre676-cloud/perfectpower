# Complete prime-preimage lattices and bounded saturation at five and seven

## What the extension supplies

The concurrent five/seven base implementation, its version-two subgroup packets and good-reduction obstruction are preserved. This extension adds independent normal-form replay, actual free-subgroup indices and an additional monic-polynomial root obstruction. The existing rational quintuple and septuple fibres now feed the complete subgroup-preimage and repeated-saturation operations. Every projective coefficient line is retained, including combinations invisible to individual-generator division. Exact row-Hermite and Smith presentations describe the coefficient lattice; actual subgroup indices are separately certified when independent free witness coordinates are supplied. The implementation supports generalized rational Weierstrass models and preserves the old double/triple interfaces and acceptance of historical packet schemas.

This push also introduces a finite-prime root obstruction for empty odd-prime fibres. It makes the mixed-generator examples practical without changing their completeness claim. Twenty worked packets exercise hidden five/seven relations, dependent inputs, rational five/seven torsion kernels, successive enlargement, positive closure witnesses, inconclusive membership and step limits. The reproduction command is `make check-elliptic-prime-subgroups`.

The general arguments in this chapter are paper proofs using the group law, division-polynomial identities and integer lattice theory. The computations use exact rational arithmetic and discovery-free Python replay. No new Lean theorem or fresh Lean build is claimed. Existing kernel-coset and group-generation foundations remain reusable for formal interpreter work.

## Rational prime fibres and a finite obstruction

The rational division engine already computes complete fibres of multiplication by five and seven. In completed short coordinates it constructs the odd division polynomial psi and the coordinate numerator phi. The kernel has complete rational-root evidence. A finite target fibre is either an exact anchor translated by that complete kernel or an empty fibre with complete obstruction evidence. Replay independently reconstructs the same polynomial in the original x-coordinate using the Weierstrass b-invariants and checks the actual multiplication equation, including the sign of the original y-coordinate.

The finite-target division equation is monic of degree p². Suppose its coefficients and the completed cubic are integral at an odd prime ell. A rational root must be ell-integral: if its valuation were negative, the leading monic term would have strictly smaller valuation than every other term, so their sum could not vanish. Its completed y-coordinate is ell-integral as well, since its square is an ell-integral cubic value. Every rational preimage therefore reduces to an equation root for which the completed cubic is a square modulo ell.

$$
f_p(x)=0,\quad Y^2=C(x)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f_p(\bar x)=0,\quad C(\bar x)\ \mathrm{is\ a\ square\ modulo}\ \ell.
$$

The new obstruction enumerates every root residue modulo a prime between five and 199 and proves that every corresponding cubic value is a nonzero quadratic nonresidue. The empty root set is allowed. This is an exact exclusion theorem, not a bounded search for rational roots. Primes dividing coefficient denominators are skipped. A failed local search proves nothing and falls through to the existing anchor search and complete rational-root fallback. The check does not need a good-reduction group-homomorphism argument; it uses only monicity, rational valuation and the two necessary polynomial equations.

The new optional `local_obstruction` evidence exists only for the `local_root_obstruction` method. Historical prime-fibre packets remain accepted with their original field sets. Replay verifies primality, coefficient denominators, the complete residue root table and all Legendre symbols. It calls neither the obstruction producer nor root discovery. The root-node budget still counts Sturm nodes; the new finite check has a fixed small prime cap and its own checker work charges.

## Every residue combination, including hidden ones

Let G be the rational point group and let Gamma be generated by P_1 through P_r. Divisibility of a coefficient combination depends only on its coefficients modulo p. The complete relation space is the kernel of the map from the finite coefficient vector space into G/pG.

$$
W=\{a\in\mathbb{F}_p^r:\ \sum_i a_iP_i\in pG\},\qquad \Lambda=\{a\in\mathbb{Z}^r:\ a\ \mathrm{mod}\ p\in W\}.
$$

A nonzero scalar multiple represents the same divisibility question. The service tests every nonzero projective line, normalized by its first nonzero coefficient being one, plus the complete rational p-kernel. There are (p^r−1)/(p−1) such lines. A complete fibre supplies membership; an exact empty fibre supplies nonmembership. Reduced row echelon form gives a basis of W. The replay checker expands every scalar multiple of every successful line and independently compares that full finite set with the proposed basis span. It rejects missing lines, altered targets and changed shared budget allocations.

On y²=x³−4x+1, take P=(0,1) and Q=(2,1). For p equal to five or seven, supply A=P+Q and B=(p−1)P−Q. Neither A nor B is p-divisible over the rationals. Their sum is pP, so testing the generators alone misses the enlargement. The complete relation basis is (1,1), and the replacement points are P and B. This is checked using six projective lines at five and eight at seven. The nondivisible lines are closed by finite root obstructions, not by assumed independence or a point-height cutoff.

## Canonical lattice forms and actual generator equations

Lift the RREF entries to integers between zero and p−1. For a pivot column j, take the corresponding relation row as H_j. For a nonpivot column j, take H_j=p e_j. Place these rows in their original column order. This is the upper-triangular row-Hermite convention: positive pivots, zeros below them and entries above each pivot reduced modulo that pivot. The diagonal entries are one at relation pivots and p elsewhere.

Subtract each off-pivot relation coefficient times its pivot column from the corresponding nonpivot column. This gives a unimodular column matrix C with H C diagonal. Moving pivot rows and columns first gives explicit unimodular U and V and the divisibility-ordered Smith diagonal. The service returns the matrices, not only a claimed diagonal.

$$
UHV=\operatorname{diag}(1,\ldots,1,p,\ldots,p),\qquad [\mathbb{Z}^r:\Lambda]=p^{r-d}.
$$

Here d is the relation-space dimension. The checker independently verifies integer determinants, unimodularity, matrix multiplication, Hermite inequalities, residue-span inclusion and the lattice index. Inclusion plus the independently determined index proves equality with the desired coefficient lattice. A separate SymPy census compares Smith factors and tests membership through the inverse Hermite matrix at every finite residue coordinate.

For each pivot row choose its exact rational division anchor as the replacement point Q_j. For nonpivot rows keep P_j. The certificates verify pQ_j=sum_i H_ji P_i and express every original source point through the replacements. The complete rational p-kernel is retained separately. For any point R in the subgroup preimage, write pR=sum_i a_iP_i with a in Lambda, express a in the H basis, and subtract the corresponding replacement combination from R. The remainder is killed by p. Conversely, every replacement and every retained kernel point maps into Gamma. This proves that the output generators generate the entire rational prime-preimage subgroup without independence or torsion assumptions.

In the mixed example, H has rows (1,1) and (0,p), the Smith diagonal is (1,p), and source containment is A=pP−B. Zero generators, repeated generators and the empty source list remain supported. Empty source input returns all rational p-torsion; the worked five- and seven-torsion curves return complete kernels of sizes five and seven.

## Coefficient indices are not subgroup indices

The cardinality of W is p^d. It is not generally the index of the actual enlarged subgroup. For duplicate source generators P,P, the relation (1,p−1) is present even when their subgroup is already p-saturated. The presentation correctly reports its coefficient-lattice data while leaving `actual_subgroup_index` unset. Dependence and torsion are not silently treated as free independent generators.

The separate actual-index operation requires a certified independent witness tuple modulo torsion, square integer coordinate matrices S and T for the old sources and new replacement points, and a trivial rational p-kernel. Exact point equations bind those matrices to the actual elliptic points. Independence makes their witness span a free abelian group. The two full-rank lattices have index ratio given by their integer determinants.

$$
[\langle Q_1,\ldots,Q_r\rangle:\langle P_1,\ldots,P_r\rangle]=\frac{|\det S|}{|\det T|}.
$$

The complete preimage certificate establishes containment. The ratio must be a positive integer. For the mixed five/seven examples the source coordinates are (1,1),(p−1,−1), the replacement coordinates are (1,0),(p−1,−1), and the actual index is p. The witness independence certificate is independently replayed using the existing good-reduction machinery. A dependent witness tuple, false coordinate equation, altered index or nontrivial rational prime kernel causes rejection. This free-index operation is deliberately narrower than the general subgroup-preimage operation; complete relation lattices modulo torsion remain a separate extension.

## Repeated saturation and joint closure

The existing bounded saturation engine now accepts any sorted distinct prime list from two, three, five and seven. The new presentation wrapper defaults to five and seven and attaches normal-form evidence for every completed step. Each step uses the complete preimage, never a subset of successful coefficient lines. Generator compression removes a point only with an exact integer combination witness. Bounded membership failure is inconclusive.

A prime is marked closed only when every generator of the complete preimage is witnessed inside the current subgroup. That proves equality of the groups. An actual enlargement clears all prior closure flags, since another prime must be rechecked on the larger group. Equality-preserving compression retains closure. The output status distinguishes joint closure, a step limit and a generator-width limit. Root-budget exhaustion raises; it never returns a partial division table labelled complete.

On y²+y=x³−x with P=(0,0), start from the subgroup generated by 35P. The five-stage schedule is 5,5,7,5,7. The generator is first reduced to 7P, then to P. The actual certified indices are 5,1,7,1,1. The final subgroup is simultaneously 5- and 7-saturated in E(Q). This says nothing about whether it is the full Mordell–Weil group. A one-step run instead returns a complete enlargement with `step-limit`, and a zero membership-coefficient bound returns an unresolved closure question rather than a false nonmembership result.

| Worked family | Complete conclusion |

## Reproduction and implementation boundaries

Run `make check-elliptic-prime-subgroups`. It regenerates twenty exact worked packets, crosschecks eighty finite lattice presentations, examines 28,656 residue coordinates and verifies forty rational multiplication-coordinate identities against the independently constructed original-model division polynomials. Known divisible points are checked against the new local exclusion to ensure no false obstruction. Fourteen focused tests cover torsion, mixed relations, dependence, old primes, limits, corruption, cold service reload and discovery-free replay. All 80 merged elliptic tests pass. The command also runs the complete elliptic test family, including historical doubling, tripling, composed division, subgroup and saturation tests.

Inputs retain the existing four-generator limit, shared root-node budget at most 100000, coefficient budget 4096 bits for five/seven fibres, at most sixteen saturation stages and bounded membership search. Normal-form and actual-index coordinate entries are limited to absolute value 256; the actual-index witness width is one through four. Requests outside the supported arithmetic or packet budgets fail explicitly. Root discovery can propose an anchor but cannot establish emptiness. Rank lower bounds certify witness independence, not global rank upper bounds.

The main components are `elliptic_subgroups.py`, `elliptic_subgroup_verifier.py`, `elliptic_prime_division.py`, `elliptic_prime_division_verifier.py`, `elliptic_lattice_presentation.py` and `elliptic_lattice_verifier.py`. The public curve methods `subgroup_preimage` and `bounded_saturation` gain five/seven support. New methods `subgroup_presentation` and `saturation_presentation` return normal forms. The JSON dispatcher supplies corresponding replay operations and the separate actual-index operation. Historical explicit prime-division, chart, wall and flavor APIs are preserved.

The next arithmetic target is maximal-order and nonmonic norm transport, or complete relation presentations including torsion so that actual indices can be certified beyond the current free-coordinate scope. The general packet interpreter, division recurrence semantics and implementation-to-Lean refinements remain formal obligations. This push closes bounded complete five/seven subgroup preimages and saturation within the documented scope; it supplies no new Mordell rank upper bound or global integral-point bound.
