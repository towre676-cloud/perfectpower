## Exact scalar poles and canonical residues

On the stationary Gaussian valley S_a = -J_a/M_a squared, write H for the source/Higgs Hessian and D_ai = partial_i J_a. The full canonical UV Hessian has blocks H + D-transpose diag(1/M_a squared) D, D-transpose, D, and diag(M_a squared). Its static Schur complement is exactly H, so its stationary energies and the signs of its light static curvatures reproduce the original source potential. Its finite pole masses are different because heavy fields carry canonical kinetic terms.

For a timelike squared momentum z below every mediator threshold, eliminating the heavy fluctuation gives F(z) = H - z K(z), where K(z) = I + D-transpose diag[1/(M_a squared (M_a squared - z))] D. The full characteristic determinant factors into det[diag(M_a squared) - z I] det F(z). Exact symbolic checks establish the one-source identity for general h,d,M,z and a polynomial identity with two source coordinates and three unequal mediators. The general formula follows directly from the same block Schur complement. No field-to-heavy-mass expansion is used for the pole equation.

The two-derivative tree metric is K(0) = I + D-transpose diag(1/M_a to the fourth) D. At a root z, the physical residue metric is Z(z) = -partial_z F(z) = I + D-transpose diag[1/(M_a squared-z) squared] D. If F(z)v = 0, its heavy component is -diag[1/(M_a squared-z)] Dv. The full vector is canonically normalized precisely when v-transpose Z(z) v = 1. Distinct full eigenvectors are orthogonal even though their projected source vectors use different pole metrics. Degenerate source modes are handled by the full nullspace basis; individual eigenvectors inside a degenerate subspace have no invariant identity.

These formulas distinguish a zero-momentum curvature, a local derivative approximation and a finite UV pole. For a positive source Hessian whose largest eigenvalue is below the smallest mediator squared mass, every ordered root obeys lower_bound_i <= exact_pole_i <= two_derivative_pole_i <= bare_curvature_i. A useful lower bound divides the ordered eigenvalue of H by 1 + norm(D) squared/[M_min squared (M_min squared - lambda_max(H))]. Here norm(D) is the spectral norm. It follows from the positive matrix bound on K(z) and the min-max principle. The momentum-dependent eigenvalues of F(z) decrease strictly because -partial_z F is positive; each branch therefore has one root in the stated below-threshold interval. The numerical solver independently checks the resulting roots, full canonical vectors and collective light-subspace projector against the full matrix.

## What first-order mass-phase reality does and does not enforce

For a canonical triangular fermion matrix D = (a I,0; C,M I), C Hermitian and a,M real positive, each neutral real-scalar vertex has the form G_alpha = (b_alpha I,0; H_alpha,0), with b_alpha real and H_alpha Hermitian. The established paired-vertex theorem gives Im Tr(D-inverse delta D) = 0 for arbitrary real scalar mixing and positive scalar masses. This remains a first-order statement in the one-loop mass correction.

Every corrected vertex contribution still has zero right column block, so the determinant of D + delta D depends only on its corrected upper-left three-by-three block. In the basis diagonalizing C, a real spectral kernel f_alpha(D D-dagger) has upper contractions L_alpha(C) and K_alpha(C), both real functions of C. The upper mass correction is proportional to the sum of b_alpha squared L_alpha(C) + b_alpha K_alpha(C) H_alpha. The second term need not be Hermitian because its two Hermitian factors need not commute. Its trace is real; its higher trace powers need not be.

An exact resolvent kernel makes this distinction algebraically transparent. For f_s(X) = (X+s I)-inverse, L_s = a(M squared+s)/den and K_s = s C/den, with den = (a squared+s)(M squared+s)+s C squared. Take C = diag(2/5,7/10,11/10), a = 1/10, M = 1 and scalar vertices consisting of a Higgs coefficient 1/5, a real 12 off-diagonal source coefficient 1/5 and an imaginary Hermitian 12 source coefficient 1/5. Mix their three scalar fields with the rational orthogonal matrix having rows (1,2,2)/3, (2,1,-2)/3 and (2,-2,1)/3; the squared masses are 3/10,4/5,7/5. The linear determinant phase coefficient vanishes exactly. The quadratic coefficient is the nonzero rational -5152/727803505 before the loop-normalization factor. Exact expansion of the full three-by-three determinant verifies the coefficient.

The resolvent is a structural example, not a substitute for the physical loop function. Evaluating the actual finite B0 function for the same data gives a quadratic determinant phase -4.480088664908940366 times 10 to the minus 10 radians and a resummed phase -4.481809518794191284 times 10 to the minus 10 radians. The full six-by-six SVD calculation agrees with an independent 80-digit divided-difference evaluation of the upper mass block. Its relative correction norm is below 0.000533; its first-order phase is zero analytically. Formal scaling of the one-loop correction verifies the quadratic approach to the zero-correction limit.

The independent four-scalar example imposes an additional structural restriction: the Higgs Hessian row couples only to a diagonal source, with zero direct Higgs mixing into either off-diagonal source. The diagonal source mixes with the real and imaginary off-diagonal sources in the remaining positive Hessian. The first-order phase is below 10 to the minus 16, while the resummed one-loop matrix phase is approximately 1.4070541 times 10 to the minus 10 with relative correction norm below 0.02. This shows that a Higgs row aligned to a commuting source direction is not alone enough to extend the first-order theorem through propagation across the scalar sector.

Both examples are spectral tests. A complete invariant scalar potential with those Hessians, its stationary vacuum and all gauge/fermion sectors have not been supplied. More importantly, the quadratic determinant term of a one-loop-corrected matrix is only one contribution at the next perturbative order. Genuine two-loop self energies and the remaining matching terms can contribute at that order. These examples disprove the inference that first-order reality automatically protects the resummed determinant; they do not calculate a complete two-loop strong-CP prediction for the repository model.

## A protective candidate with an exact running test

If no scalar mass eigenmode contains both a Higgs vertex and a source vertex, source modes have b_alpha = 0 and do not correct the top block. Higgs modes have H_alpha = 0 and correct it only by real functions L_alpha(C). The corrected top block is then Hermitian and commutes with C. Its determinant ratio remains real and positive whenever the normalized correction norm is below one. This protects every power in that restricted determinant resummation. It remains a statement about one-loop matrices; it does not address all diagrams at arbitrary loop order.

The candidate separation is not stable under the stated Yukawa interactions. At zero Higgs portals, pure source quartics and the Higgs self quartic share no scalar-loop product, while off-diagonal source wavefunctions cannot generate a source/Higgs product from those separated potentials. The colored fermion box does generate them. For each real charge sector with Higgs coefficient y, source singlet coefficients e,t and adjoint coefficient a, its four source-quadratic portal beta coefficients are -36 y squared e squared, -36 y squared t squared, -72 y squared e t and -12 y squared a squared. These are 16 pi squared-normalized coefficients multiplying H-dagger H times eta squared, s squared, eta s and the adjoint norm respectively.

All four vanish exactly only if y = 0 or e = t = a = 0 in that sector. The diagonal coefficients establish this directly by positivity for real couplings. Nonzero Higgs masses together with nonzero source mixing therefore prevent this zero-portal subspace from closing under the specified running. Ordinary phase charges cannot forbid these neutral products while allowing their factors. Additional particles, a different interaction structure or justified cancellation relations can alter the conclusion; none is supplied by setting these portals to zero as a boundary input. The new result specifies exactly what a protection mechanism must change.
