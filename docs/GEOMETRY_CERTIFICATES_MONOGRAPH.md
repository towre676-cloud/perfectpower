# Exact meshes and all-walk routing certificates

**The practical change.** PerfectPower can now accept a triangle mesh without rerunning its ear-clipping constructor, and accept a visibility-graph route without rerunning its shortest-path search. These are useful distinctions. A producer chooses a decomposition or route; an independent acceptance function establishes the properties that matter. The new route emitter proves a bound for every finite walk in the retained graph. The mesh emitter establishes actual triangle separation, coordinate-derived atomic boundary cancellation and area equality. Full polygon topology and geometric visibility still have their stated Python scope. This extension builds on the Belyaev-inspired rational geometry release rather than claiming to formalize another chapter of the book.

**Why area equality was an insufficient stopping point.** A square of side four has area sixteen. Two positive triangles of area eight each can overlap and leave part of that square uncovered. Their summed area is still sixteen. Accordingly, equality of the area sum is necessary but cannot establish that the triangles constitute a valid mesh. The preceding release generated sensible ear decompositions and exercised coverage with sampled point queries. The new checker adds exact finite separation and boundary conditions and applies to supplied decompositions, including ones that the previous constructor would never emit.

The retained rejection example uses the triangles with vertices `(0,0),(4,0),(4,4)` and `(0,0),(4,0),(0,4)`. Each has area eight. Their strict interiors overlap. The checker rejects the pair by testing separating axes, despite the perfectly matching total. A second rejection replaces a missing half-square triangle with an equally sized triangle outside the source. That pair has no interior overlap but fails the boundary condition. These examples exercise different failure modes instead of relying on one numerical invariant.

**Mesh admission.** The source is a simple, nonzero-area rational polygon without holes. Existing exact polygon admission rejects side crossings, nonadjacent contact, backtracking, repeated vertices and zero area. Clockwise input is reversed. Every supplied triangle must have three vertices and strictly positive counterclockwise determinant. Coordinates are integers or rational strings; binary floating-point inputs are rejected. A repeated closing source vertex remains admissible under the preceding polygon convention. A mesh may add rational Steiner vertices and may use either diagonal of a quadrilateral. The acceptance contract is about its geometry, rather than identity with a preferred constructor's output.

For each pair of triangles, the checker searches the lines supporting their six sides. A witness records a line through two actual rational points and a sign. All vertices of the first triangle must lie in one closed halfplane and all vertices of the second in the opposite closed halfplane. The separating-axis criterion is exact for convex triangles. Shared vertices and shared sides are allowed, while an overlap of positive area has no such witness. The output retains one separator for every triangle pair, so the acceptance data can be inspected or emitted as source-bound arithmetic.

Write the oriented side expression as

\[
 L_{a,b}(p)=(b_x-a_x)(p_y-a_y)-(b_y-a_y)(p_x-a_x).
\]

A retained separator with sign \(s\in\{1,-1\}\) satisfies \(sL_{a,b}(p)\geq0\) at every first-triangle vertex and \(sL_{a,b}(q)\leq0\) at every second-triangle vertex. A convex combination preserves these inequalities. The generic Lean module proves the corresponding homogeneous-coordinate identity and its nonnegative-weight consequence after rational denominators are cleared. It also proves that a point cannot occupy both strict sides. The finite emitted sources check the actual vertex inequalities. There is no assertion that one vertex happens to be separated from the other triangle while the rest is ignored.

**Boundary cancellation handles subdivisions.** Merely counting unsplit triangle sides fails on a T-junction. One triangle can present a long edge while two neighbouring triangles present its two halves. Those sides describe the same geometric interface but have different endpoint pairs. The new checker collects all source and triangle vertices, splits every edge at every admitted collinear vertex, and counts the resulting oriented atomic segments. Lexicographically ordered endpoints give each atom a fixed direction. A reversed occurrence contributes minus one. Interior atoms cancel; surviving coefficients must equal those obtained from the source polygon boundary.

This procedure also accepts source boundary subdivisions that are absent from the triangle vertices. An extra source point halfway along a straight square side is a legitimate polygon vertex. The triangle mesh can retain the full side. Both are split at the same admitted point and receive the same atomic boundary. Conversely, removing a triangle or placing an equal-area patch elsewhere changes the oriented chain and is rejected. A supplied positive-area mesh cannot silently replace the source by another shape with the same scalar area.

Let \(c_T(e)\) and \(c_P(e)\) denote signed triangle and polygon multiplicities on atomic edge \(e\). The acceptance conditions include

\[
 c_T(e)=c_P(e)\quad\text{for every admitted atom},\qquad
 \det(T_i)>0,\qquad
 \sum_i\frac{\det(T_i)}2=\operatorname{area}(P).
\]

Combined with exact triangle separation and the admitted simple-polygon topology, these conditions characterize the filled mesh used by the executable layer. A full Jordan-curve, winding and coverage theorem is still outside Lean. That distinction remains explicit: a theorem checking all retained boundary arithmetic is stronger than an area receipt, but does not itself constitute a formal development of planar topology.

**What Lean computes for the mesh.** Positive denominators are cleared to integer coordinates. The source contains the actual sorted coordinate list, polygon edges and triangle edges. Lean checks that the coordinates are strictly ordered, that edge endpoints belong to the list, each positive triangle determinant, the source polygon area, the triangle area sum, and every supplied separating halfplane. Importantly, atomic subdivisions are recomputed inside Lean by exact closed-segment membership, ordering and endpoint-index lookup. The boundary comparison covers all pairs of admitted indices, including pairs whose coefficient is zero. It does not simply trust a Python list of already cancelled counts. Finite claims use kernel-checked `decide` proofs.

The generic foundation uses `Std` and requires no Mathlib installation. Its all-walk graph theorem and weighted-side identities use the standard Lean axioms reported in the retained log. None of the accepted sources depends on `sorryAx`. Proof-emitter metadata starts with `kernel_checked: false`; a successful validation receipt separately identifies the exact source hashes that passed. Changing an emitted source after validation creates a different source and invalidates that hash association.

**The route certificate is a feasible potential.** The graph remains the full rational visibility graph on supplied endpoints and distinct obstacle vertices. A straight edge is admitted when it avoids every polygon interior; boundary contact is allowed. Each edge has rational Manhattan weight \(w(u,v)=|x_u-x_v|+|y_u-y_v|\). A certificate supplies one rational potential \(\pi(v)\) per vertex satisfying

\[
 \pi(v)\leq\pi(u)+w(u,v)
\]

for every directed edge. An undirected edge is therefore checked in both directions. Along any walk \(v_0,\ldots,v_m\), summing these inequalities yields

\[
 \pi(v_m)-\pi(v_0)\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}w(v_i,v_{i+1}).
\]

A supplied start-to-target path is optimal in this graph when its actual summed cost equals the potential difference. The acceptance function checks each path edge, coordinates, endpoints, cost and this equality. It never calls Dijkstra or repeated relaxation. The producer does use a full-distance Dijkstra pass to obtain convenient potentials, but correctness of that implementation is unnecessary for accepting a particular feasible certificate.

The generic Lean theorem defines finite walks inductively, permitting arbitrary lengths and repeated vertices. Its induction proves the potential bound for every walk, rather than checking a retained list of rival paths. A source-bound emitter clears all edge, potential and cost denominators by a positive common denominator, proves the finite edge inequalities, constructs the actual retained walk, checks the dual equality and invokes the generic optimality theorem. The retained square obstacle example has cost six. No competing finite walk in its specified graph has a lower cost.

**Certificates need not reproduce an optimizer's preferred answer.** Adding the same rational constant to every potential preserves all edge inequalities and the endpoint difference. The checker accepts such shifted potentials. An alternative path of the same cost is also accepted, even when it disagrees with the constructor's lexicographic tie-breaking rule. The existing router still uses its stated tie policy, but the new proof establishes cost optimality only. This enables independent producers, future optimizers and alternative geometric decompositions to share one acceptance contract.

The certificate also carries a source hash. A changed obstacle, vertex list, edge list, path or cost no longer matches the retained source. Even if a caller recomputes that hash after a modification, acceptance still reconstructs the complete geometric vertex and edge lists and checks the witness conditions. A feasible potential for a graph missing inconvenient edges is not accepted as a certificate for the full supplied visibility graph. Geometry replay remains Python and uses the existing exact visibility predicate; the Lean theorem's scope is the emitted finite graph.

**Unreachability is a closed-component witness.** A route packet can instead declare the target unreachable. Its certificate includes a set containing the source but excluding the target, with no graph edge crossing its boundary. The acceptance function checks this closure on the complete graph. The generic Lean induction shows that every walk starting in the set stays in it, and therefore no walk reaches the excluded target. The retained overlapping obstacle ring supplies this example. A disconnected component uses zero potentials when convenient; there are no edges from it to the source component, so that choice does not weaken the closed-set argument.

**Connecting the mesh guarantee to Voronoi output.** `certify_voronoi_mesh` accepts each supplied full-dimensional cell mesh with the independent checker. It reconstructs the source planar Voronoi diagram, checks cell labels, separation and boundary data, and recomputes area and centroid from the accepted positive triangles. Empty, point and segment cells have explicit dimension and zero-area contracts. Coincident site labels may even carry different accepted decompositions of their shared cell; the total area counts their geometry once. The adapter rejects corrupt moments and doubled duplicate-site area. Voronoi halfplane clipping and the source diagram's geometric correctness remain Python rather than becoming formal by association with a cell certificate.

**Running and reusing the extension.** `PYTHONPATH=python python3 -m perfectpower geometry-foundations mesh-certificate examples/geometry_certificates/mesh.json` produces an independent mesh packet. The `mesh-proof` operation emits its Lean source and metadata. `route-certificate` returns a route and dual witness; `route-proof` adds the source-bound Lean proof. `voronoi-mesh-certificate` combines the existing Voronoi mesh producer with independent acceptance. The callable Python functions also accept external packets directly, so users need not take the CLI producer's decomposition as authoritative.

`make geometry-certificates` regenerates the retained examples and runs the new tests. `make geometry-certificates-kernel` checks the generic module and eight emitted sources using `PERFECTPOWER_LEAN` when an explicit executable is needed. These include a four-triangle Steiner fan, a T-junction, a concave polygon, a rationally rotated mesh, the square-obstacle route, a zero-length route, a rational-coordinate route and an unreachable component. Five deliberate mutations must fail by Lean's arithmetic `decide` tactic: an infeasible potential, a false exclusion from the closed component, a wrong area, a reversed separator and an extra boundary edge. Missing imports or tool failures cannot count as successful rejection evidence.

The targeted run has seventeen new test methods and fifty-four preceding geometry, Euclidean, linear-product and graph regression methods, for seventy-one passing tests. New tests include thirty rational Steiner fans, thirty route endpoint cases compared against independent Bellman–Ford relaxation, fifteen random Voronoi diagrams, rational rigid transformations and exact winding-based grid comparisons for concave meshes. Mocked search and ear-clipping functions establish that acceptance does not accidentally call those producers. These are retained execution tests, not a public performance benchmark or a proof of every possible Python run.

**Boundaries and value.** Work limits raise without returning partial objects as complete. Mesh checks have separate triangle and vertex caps; visibility checks retain a graph vertex cap. Counts measure predicate and algorithm stages, not integer bit complexity, memory or elapsed time. The route objective remains Manhattan cost on a finite graph. It does not establish a global Euclidean shortest path, arbitrary intermediate bends, positive clearance, finite-radius string physics or a physical robot trajectory. Manhattan cost is axis dependent, so a rational Euclidean rotation can preserve obstacle geometry while changing the optimization objective.

The useful architectural gain is that mesh construction and route optimization can now vary while acceptance remains stable. A faster producer can propose a different triangulation, use Steiner points, select a different equal-cost route or obtain potentials by a different method. It must still satisfy the same explicit geometry and finite arithmetic. PerfectPower gains transferable certificates for spatial objects, source-bound all-walk cost theorems and a direct connection to its planar Voronoi output. Full Lean polygon coverage, formally derived visibility edges and positive-clearance geometric planning are the remaining substantive bridges.
