# Polynomial wall responses and the formal radiation-cancellation hierarchy

The retuned wall calculation now predicts the curvature of its radiation-node condition from five forced linear responses. It no longer needs to infer that coefficient from the nonlinear candidate curve. In the decoupled-singlet case, the source quartic and Higgs vacuum ratio enter the result through an explicit universal scaling law. Independent coupled background/eigenmode calculations check that law at three parameter choices. Independent outgoing-pole calculations change the observed small-portal leakage from sixth-power to eighth-power behavior when the new quadratic correction is applied. The deeper mathematical extension is an order-by-order Fredholm construction: parity leaves one real open-channel solvability condition, and the next Higgs-quartic coefficient enters it with a nonzero overlap. This constructs a formal localized continuation, without establishing convergence or infinite-domain existence.

The declared action and normalization. Work in rho=v z, with u the dimensionless odd source, h the dimensionless even Higgs radius, fixed h0=v_H/v>0, source quartic lambda>0, and portal kappa. Set the Gaussian mediator coupling to zero, so that the spectator singlet decouples exactly. The square-completed potential, divided by v to the fourth, is

$$V=\frac{\lambda}{4}(u^2-1)^2+\frac{\lambda_H}{4}\left[h^2-h_0^2+\frac{\kappa}{\lambda_H}(u^2-1)\right]^2.$$

At kappa=0, put k=sqrt(lambda/2), x=k rho, u=tanh(x), s=sech(x), and p=s tanh(x). The radiation-node Higgs quartic is lambda_H0=k squared/h0 squared. The source shape energy is 3k squared; its dimensionless odd mode is p/k, normalized to source derivative one at rho=0. The open Higgs wave number is k, and the first induced Higgs mode is kappa h0 s/k cubed. Both modes decay even though the Higgs channel is energetically open. The previous release derived the first threshold correction C approximately -1.36468352107739. The present calculation keeps the full square completion, including the kappa squared/lambda_H source terms, while expanding the background and mode consistently.

The five universal responses. Primes below denote derivatives with respect to x. H is the first Higgs background response; A is the second source background response; T is the second Higgs background response; B is the second Higgs-mode response; W is the second source-mode response. Their scaling definitions are

$$h=h_0+\frac{\kappa h_0}{k^2}H+\frac{\kappa^2 h_0}{k^4}T+\cdots,\qquad u=\tanh x+\frac{\kappa^2 h_0^2}{k^4}A+\cdots.$$

$$\psi=\frac{p}{k}+\frac{\kappa^2 h_0^2}{k^5}W+\cdots,\qquad \chi=\frac{\kappa h_0}{k^3}s+\frac{\kappa^2 h_0}{k^5}B+\cdots.$$

The response H satisfies H-double-prime=2H-s squared, with even parity and decay. The source response A satisfies A-double-prime=(4-6s squared)A+tanh(x)(2H-s squared), with odd parity and decay. The second static Higgs response satisfies T-double-prime=2T+3H squared+CH-Hs squared. The second open mode satisfies B-double-prime+B=s[(8-2s squared)H+C-s squared]. Requiring B to be even and decaying fixes C. This is the same cosine solvability condition as the earlier Green-response calculation, now appearing inside the coupled response hierarchy. No outgoing width enters these equations or the determination of C.

The source-mode Fredholm condition. The odd shape operator has kernel p. Its order-two response satisfies

$$W''=(1-6s^2)W+(12uA+2H+3u^2-1-e)p+2us,\qquad u=\tanh x.$$

Odd parity requires W(0)=0. The mode normalization requires W-prime(0)=0, removing the arbitrary addition of p. Decay supplies the remaining solvability condition and determines e. Independently, multiply the equation by p and integrate by parts over the real line. Since the norm of p squared is exactly 2/3, the scalar moment is

$$e=\frac{3}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p^2(12uA+2H+3u^2+1)\,dx.$$

The implementation obtains e both as a boundary-problem parameter and through this independent Fredholm moment. Their numerical values agree at about fourteen decimal places in the executed calculation. The physical energy expansion, divided by v squared, is E=3lambda/2+(2h0 squared/lambda)e kappa squared+O(kappa cubed). Thus the source background distortion, the virtual Higgs response and the source mode all contribute to the same derived energy coefficient. Keeping the source mode unchanged would miss one of the ingredients needed for the next radiation cancellation.

The third-order open-channel forcing. Let the unmixed Higgs threshold be m_H squared/v squared=lambda+C kappa+Q kappa squared+O(kappa cubed). Expanding the canonical Hessian and mode equation gives a third-order Higgs source consisting of a Higgs-only piece and a piece proportional to h0 squared. With u=tanh(x), these universal forcing functions are

$$f_H=2uTp+(6H+C-s^2)B+s(6T+3H^2+3CH),$$

$$f_S=2uW+2Ap-es.$$

The remaining adjustable term is Q k squared times s, after the common factor h0/k to the fifth is removed. Its cosine overlap is nonzero. Put F=pi/cosh(pi/2), D_H=-integral cos(x) f_H(x) dx/F, and D_S=-integral cos(x) f_S(x) dx/F. The new coefficient is

$$Q=\frac{2}{\lambda}\left(D_H+h_0^2D_S\right).$$

This separation is exact algebra in the perturbative hierarchy. Why the formal construction continues. The static source operator is minus d squared/dx squared+4-6s squared. Its translation zero mode is even, while the source corrections here are odd; the odd inverse is therefore unambiguous. The static Higgs operator minus d squared/dx squared+2 is strictly positive. The even open operator d squared/dx squared+1 admits a decaying solution precisely when its real cosine forcing moment vanishes. If that condition holds, its decaying solution is given by the terminal Green integral of sin(t-x) times the forcing from x to infinity; the cosine moment imposes the derivative-zero parity condition at the origin.

At the next portal order the new threshold coefficient multiplies the leading localized Higgs response s. Its solvability multiplier is the strictly positive transform F=pi/cosh(pi/2). Consequently one real quartic coefficient removes one real radiation condition. Lower response coefficients determine the other forcing terms. The resulting triangular recursion first determines the next mode radiation cancellation, then the static Higgs response that depends on that already selected quartic coefficient. The source static correction and source energy/mode correction use previously selected Higgs data. All finite-order forcings are smooth, localized combinations of earlier responses. The inverses and moment constraints above therefore specify a unique formal coefficient hierarchy after parity, fixed vacuum and mode normalization have been chosen. The statement concerns formal perturbation coefficients; it supplies neither bounds controlling their growth nor convergence of their sum.

The leakage-order consequence. The scalar-node first induced Higgs response is already localized, so its order-one radiation amplitude vanishes. Leaving the threshold uncorrected permits an order-two radiation amplitude and a fourth-power width. Correcting C removes that amplitude; correcting Q removes the order-three amplitude. A correction through threshold order n therefore formally leaves radiation beginning at order n+2 and width beginning at order 2n+4, provided the first remaining amplitude is nonzero and the outgoing normalization remains regular. This is an asymptotic mechanism derived from the amplitude equation. It does not say that every finite portal has precisely that pure power, or that a formally canceled infinite series defines a stable particle.
