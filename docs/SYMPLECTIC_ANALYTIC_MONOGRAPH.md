# From sheeted surface cycles to analytic period matrices

The program now computes a numerical symplectic period matrix of an actual squarefree hyperelliptic curve, rather than merely accepting an array of proposed periods. It starts from the existing curve-derived sheeted triangulation, constructs an integral homology basis, realizes its dual cycles as paths in the complex curve, integrates the holomorphic forms with continued sheets, and normalizes the resulting A and B blocks. The same integration machinery supports arbitrary finite nonsingular paths on normalized cyclic components, including repeated-root inputs. Period normalization also provides canonical conformal metric evaluations and Abel–Jacobi coordinates. These are implemented calculations, with generated results on curves of genera one through four. They remain numerical analytic calculations: exact integer topology has not made floating root finding or quadrature rigorous.

For an outsider, the point is that a polynomial equation can be studied as a surface with several sheets. Moving around certain points exchanges the sheets, and some paths wind around genuine holes of the surface. Integrating along those paths measures the surface's complex shape. Previously the program could describe those holes exactly and separately evaluate some contour integrals. This release connects them: every entry of the new period matrices is tied to a recorded topological cycle on the covering curve. The periods then supply a coordinate system on the Jacobian, a complex torus that packages integrals and degree-zero divisors. That is useful geometric infrastructure for studying Diophantine curves, but it does not itself decide which points have integer coordinates.

## The represented curve and its forms

For the hyperelliptic pipeline the input is a connected squarefree equation y²=P(x) of positive genus. The existing exact differential packet identifies a monic component equation z²=R(x) and an ordered basis of holomorphic forms. For squarefree R of degree m, these are x^j dx/z, with j=0,...,g−1 and g=floor((m−1)/2). Leading coefficients and component normalization are handled in that packet. All period and path routines use the normalized component coordinate, and all comparisons must preserve this convention.

The mesh builder already glues two copies of ordinary cells using the sheet permutations. The new metadata records which faces use the finite x chart and which use the inverse chart w=1/x. The faces at infinity are therefore actual analytic chart data for integration, rather than merely combinatorial caps. This metadata is additive: old mesh receipts retain their original meaning, but the analytic cycle integrator requires a newly generated mesh containing the chart labels. It refuses an old mesh without those labels.

An integral basis is obtained through the existing tree/cotree construction. Its generators are based dual loops, each described by a sequence of face crossings. The integer intersection matrix Ω and the integer change of basis S satisfy SᵀΩS=J, with J=[[0,I],[-I,0]]. Smith operation transcripts and an integral inverse of S are retained. These equalities concern the supplied finite oriented triangulation. Identifying that triangulation with the intended curve still depends on numerically computed separated roots and checked sheet gluing; this release does not supply a rigorous root-isolation proof for that identification.

## Realizing the cycles analytically

Each lifted triangle receives an interior representative. In finite faces this is a weighted barycenter in x; in inverse faces it is a weighted barycenter in w. A regular finite lifted vertex anchors the correct sheet, and its logarithm of R is analytically continued to the face representative. An ordinary shared edge is crossed at its finite midpoint. An edge incident to infinity is crossed at the corresponding nonzero inverse-coordinate midpoint, which has finite x. Consequently the integration paths avoid both ramification points and explicit infinity endpoints even when their topological cycles travel through the cap.

A crossing consists of a segment inside the source chart and a segment inside the target chart. For a finite chart segment x(t)=a+t(b−a), the continued logarithm increment is the sum over roots r of log((x(t)−r)/(a−r)). In an inverse chart, w(t)=w_a+t(w_b−w_a), and each root contributes log((1−r w(t))/(1−r w_a))−log(w(t)/w_a). The second term accounts for the pole of x=1/w; omitting it would give incorrect sheet transport at infinity. The corresponding differential factor is dx/dt=b−a in a finite chart and −(w_b−w_a)/w(t)² in an inverse chart.

For each straight chart segment, the implementation computes its distance from the chart singularities and refuses a path that meets or nearly meets one. The branch logarithms are analytically continued along the segment, rather than reselected pointwise. At the target face, the continued square root is compared with the root anchored by that actual lifted face. A mismatch rejects the crossing. This check is important because a closed projected path need not be a closed path on the cover. All accepted generator cycles have every crossing matched to the recorded target sheet.

Repeated crossings are cached with their orientation. A reverse crossing contributes the exact negative of its cached forward numerical value. This both avoids redundant integration and cancels shared tree portions consistently. Each complete generator period is the sum of its directed crossing integrals. The output retains the explicit dual walk through its basis receipt, the period vector, a sum of numerical quadrature estimates, and a minimum chart-clearance diagnostic. Those estimates are not rigorous enclosures of root or quadrature error.

The surface orientation is matched to the complex orientation of the first chart. If the chosen combinatorial orientation is reversed, the integral basis is reconstructed with all face signs reversed. The implementation does not choose a sign merely to make the final period matrix look positive. Sheet continuation, chart orientation and the intersection convention are handled before the analytic diagnostics are evaluated.

## Normalization and bilinear checks

Place the g differential integrals along the 2g recorded generators in the columns of a matrix P. The true symplectic period array is PS. Its first g columns form A and the remaining g form B. The normalized period matrix is τ=A⁻¹B. This is the standard construction, not a new theorem. Molin and Neurohr's [Computing period matrices and the Abel-Jacobi map of superelliptic curves](https://arxiv.org/html/1707.07249v1) gives a broader and rigorous arbitrary-precision treatment, including higher cyclic powers and interval arithmetic. Our implementation currently connects our own triangulated sheet model to numerical SciPy integration; it does not match that paper's rigorous numerical guarantees.

The output checks symmetry of τ and positivity of its imaginary part. It also evaluates the first bilinear relation directly in the unreduced generator basis: P Ω⁻¹ Pᵀ should vanish. This checks consistency between the original intersection matrix and the integrated cycles, independently of the A normalization. With the convention J above, the Hermitian form −i P Ω⁻¹ P* should be positive definite. Its eigenvalues are recorded as a second diagnostic. The sign is fixed by the intersection convention; replacing it with +i would reverse positivity.

The numerical routine refuses a matrix with a symmetry residual above 10⁻⁷ or a nonpositive imaginary part. Passing that threshold is a quality check, not an analytic certificate. The generated corpus has substantially smaller residuals, near floating roundoff. The condition number of A is retained because period normalization can magnify integral errors when a basis is ill-conditioned. No interval enclosure for A⁻¹ or τ is supplied.

## General continued paths

The `integrate-path` command works on a normalized cyclic component z^n=R(x), where R may have repeated root multiplicities. Its logarithm increments weight each root by its multiplicity. The exact packet supplies the correct numerator and denominator character of every holomorphic form. The numerical integrand is N_j(x) exp(−j log R(x)/n) dx, with the same continuously tracked logarithm throughout the path.

The caller supplies finite points, one finite or inverse chart for each segment, and an initial component sheet. The result includes all basis integrals, the final sheet, the continued endpoint logarithm, a projected/lifted closure distinction, and numerical diagnostics. Crossing a ramification point, passing through the inverse-chart origin, or ending at a branch point is refused. General here means arbitrary accepted nonsingular finite paths in the supported normalized cyclic model; it does not mean integration of arbitrary meromorphic integrands, arbitrary plane curves, or rigorously enclosed integrals.

This path routine is checked under path reversal and segment subdivision for squarefree hyperelliptic, cubic cyclic and repeated-root inputs. A small polygon around one simple root changes a square-root sheet; traversing it twice closes the lift and gives the contractible local contour integral zero to numerical tolerance. Finite and inverse-chart realizations of an exterior path are compared. These tests examine continuation and the actual integral, rather than only checking an array's shape.

## Canonical conformal metric

The earlier conformal metric was the sum of squared norms of a chosen monic algebraic differential basis. The periods now allow the canonical Bergman metric. Write η=A⁻¹ω for the A-normalized differential vector and Y=Im τ. In a regular finite chart its density is ρ=η* Y⁻¹ η. With dA=(i/2)ρ dx∧dbarx, the total area is g. This normalization is stated explicitly; it differs from a genus-one flat metric whose fundamental parallelogram has area Im τ.

For constant Hermitian weighting Y⁻¹, let D=η' * Y⁻¹η' and C=η*Y⁻¹η'. The Gaussian curvature is K=−2(ρD−|C|²)/ρ³. The code computes η' using the polynomial derivatives and z'/z=R'/(2R). The numerator is nonnegative in exact mathematics; any small floating violation is retained as a roundoff diagnostic before the reported curvature is clamped at zero. This is a regular finite chart evaluation. Certified branch-chart and infinity-chart values of the canonical metric are still a separate task.

A meaningful independent test changes the symplectic basis by exchanging A with B and B with −A, giving τ'=−τ⁻¹. The computed canonical density and curvature stay unchanged. This checks that the metric depends on the curve rather than the arbitrary symplectic basis. Merely calculating the same formula twice in the same basis would not test that invariance.

## Abel–Jacobi coordinates and lattice reduction

For a finite path from Q to P, normalize its integral vector by A⁻¹. The resulting u belongs to C^g modulo the lattice Z^g+τZ^g. The implementation writes u=a+τb with real vectors b=Y⁻¹ Im u and a=Re u−Re τ b. Subtracting the integer floors of a and b reduces u to the associated lattice parallelepiped. This is a coordinate reduction, not a Euclidean closest-lattice-vector calculation.

The result retains the removed A and B lattice coefficients and the unreduced normalized integral. Changing a path by an integer period combination changes u by a lattice vector and leaves the reduced point unchanged, away from numerical boundary ambiguities. A test adds 2A₁−3A₂−B₁+4B₂ to a genus-two path integral and confirms that invariance. Very large lattice coefficients are refused when floating arithmetic cannot reliably resolve their integer parts. Reduction near a parallelepiped boundary is still numerical and is not certified.

## Results and remaining frontier

The generated corpus contains six actual curves: y²=x⁴−1, y²=x⁵−x, y²=x⁵−5x³+4x, y²=x⁷−1, y²=x⁹−1, and y²=x⁵−x³−2x. Together they provide 28 generator cycles in genera one, two, three and four. All have positive imaginary period matrices and satisfy the two numerical bilinear checks. Each is rerun with different positive face-center weights and tighter quadrature tolerance while retaining the same topological cycle basis. This deformation changes the representatives of the paths, not the homology classes. Its maximum generator-period differences are recorded.

The genus-one square torus gives τ=i in the recorded basis. An independent real Euler integral checks its absolute A-period scale, not just the ratio B/A. Higher-genus symmetry, positivity, deformation invariance and the canonical metric's basis invariance provide additional independent checks. None is a proof of certified accuracy. They establish that the implemented numerical bridge behaves consistently on this corpus.

The smooth arbitrary-genus Voronoi frontier remains open: the earlier rational polyhedral enclosures do not become smooth-curve enclosures simply because the curve now has a canonical metric. They still need rigorous local metric comparisons, branch/infinity estimates, and root isolation. Rigorous period matrices likewise need validated continuation, enclosed quadrature and interval matrix inversion. Symplectic cycle integration for cyclic degree above two is also unfinished; the general path integrator supports those components, but the present closed-surface mesh and homology-to-analytic integration pipeline is hyperelliptic. No new Lean compilation is claimed in this release.

## Reproduction

Install the optional numerical dependencies from `python/requirements-analytic.txt`. Run `PYTHONPATH=python python python/build_symplectic_periods.py` to regenerate the six-curve corpus, metric samples, open paths, Abel–Jacobi reductions and deformation comparisons. Run `PYTHONPATH=python python -m unittest discover -s python/tests` for the full suite. Through the existing main module, `symplectic-periods --coeff=0,-1,0,0,0,1 --resolution 6` computes an actual genus-two symplectic period matrix. `integrate-path --coeff=-1,0,0,1 --d 3 --points '[[0.3,0.7],[0.6,0.8]]'` integrates an open path on the cubic cyclic component. The exported Python functions also expose canonical metric and Jacobian-coordinate evaluations.
