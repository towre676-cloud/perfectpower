Let gamma(q) be a polynomial path in canonically normalized real fields, with gamma(0) the supplied false vacuum and gamma(1) a lower-energy configuration. Let W(q)=V(gamma(q))-V(gamma(0)). A compact O(4) trial profile has q=1 inside a core, a smooth shell, and q=0 outside. Put u=r/R. The core is 0<=u<=L; in the shell write t=u-L between zero and one and set q(t)=1-3t^2+2t^3. This taper has zero derivative at both joins, so the profile is continuously differentiable. Outside u=L+1 the field equals the false background exactly. No exponential tail cutoff or divergent outside energy is introduced.

The complete action of this profile is

S4(R,L)=2 pi^2 [T(L) R^2 + U(L) R^4],

where T(L)=one half times the integral from zero to one of (L+t)^3 times the squared norm of d gamma(q(t))/dt. The potential term is U(L)=W(1)L^4/4 plus the integral from zero to one of (L+t)^3 W(q(t)). Both are rational polynomials in L whenever the supplied field-path and potential coefficients are rational. T has degree at most three and U has degree at most four. The core-volume contribution ensures U<0 for sufficiently large L whenever W(1)<0. This makes a negative-action escape configuration available without pretending the Euclidean bounce is a global action minimum.

For a retained shape with T>0 and U<0, the action as a function of size starts at zero, reaches a positive maximum and then becomes negative. The maximum lies at Rcrit^2=-T/(2U), with Smax/pi^2=-T^2/(2U). Its derivative under radial scaling vanishes there and its scaling second derivative is negative, as expected for the escape direction. The code evaluates these quantities as exact fractions. It uses a numerical one-parameter search only to choose a rational L and then recomputes the retained action exactly. It does not certify global optimality even within the shape family.

The constructive wall result begins with two supplied degenerate global minima and a continuously differentiable path gamma(t), 0<=t<=1, in canonically normalized real fields. Write W(t)=V(gamma(t))-Vmin and K(t)=the Euclidean squared norm of gamma'(t). Provided W is nonnegative, minimization of the one-dimensional spatial parametrization of that fixed path gives a candidate interface tension equal to the integral of sqrt(2 W K). The true infimum of the interface tension cannot exceed the value from this path. Cauchy–Schwarz then gives the useful exact squared bound

sigma_min squared <= sigma_path squared <= 2 (integral_0^1 W(t) dt) (integral_0^1 K(t) dt).

One sees the first step by introducing s=dt/dz>0 in the static energy: its density integrated over t is K s/2+W/s, minimized pointwise at s=sqrt(2W/K). Endpoint singularities or interior zeros are understood by limiting finite-energy profiles. Additional paths can lower the upper bound; no lower bound follows. The argument assumes the declared degenerate global minima. Metastable minima with a deeper third branch do not by themselves define a stable cosmological wall.

Choose an explicit positive definite coefficient metric R and a budget c transpose R c <=rho squared. The mathematical problem is to maximize |b transpose c| subject to A c=0. Discard redundant rows of A by exact elimination. Set x=R inverse b and

z=R inverse b - R inverse A transpose (A R inverse A transpose) inverse A R inverse b.

Then A z=0, z transpose R z=b transpose z>=0, and the exact maximum squared bias is rho squared times b transpose z. For a nonzero result, the maximizing coefficient vector is rho z/sqrt(b transpose z). This follows by taking the R-orthogonal projection of x onto ker A and applying Cauchy–Schwarz in the R metric. The module gives the rational projected direction, exact squared support and all constraint residuals.
