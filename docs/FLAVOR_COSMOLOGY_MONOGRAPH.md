Let C denote a consistent CP operation on the full theory and let v be a homogeneous stationary field configuration. If the scalar potential and kinetic metric obey V(Cphi)=V(phi) and the corresponding isometry relation, Cv is stationary with the same energy and the same canonically measured fluctuation spectrum. An internal-symmetry-invariant CP-odd observable O obeys O(gphi)=O(phi) and O(Cphi)=-O(phi). If O(v) is nonzero, v and Cv cannot be in the same orbit of the internal unitary group: assuming Cv=gv gives O(v)=-O(v). For a quark-matched example, a nonzero Jarlskog invariant with nondegenerate spectra is such a diagnostic. It proves orbit distinction, not a count of all global minima. Distinct orbits alone do not prove disconnected vacuum components: a continuous degenerate path could join them. Isolated global minima after gauge reduction supply the additional hypothesis. A positive physical Hessian establishes local isolation; the repository's remaining global search must establish global minimality.

There is an exact energy result independent of any numerical minimizer. In a CP-invariant quantum theory with a compatible measure, regulator and renormalization prescription, CP-even corrections preserve equality of conjugate branch energies. Thresholds, perturbative running, higher-loop terms and CP-even higher-dimensional operators cannot alone create a CP-conjugate vacuum bias. They may change the common vacuum energy, wall profile or transition order; they do not select a sign. The thermal version holds for a CP-symmetric state, including zero CP-odd chemical potentials. A baryon asymmetry, imposed CP-odd background or explicit CP-breaking spurion changes this premise and must be included as a physical input. Full quantum CP consistency for the repository's enlarged field content is not established by its restricted elementary intertwiner alone.

Write W(t)=V(gamma(t))-Vmin and K(t)=the Euclidean squared norm of gamma'(t). Provided W is nonnegative, minimization of the one-dimensional spatial parametrization of that fixed path gives a candidate interface tension equal to the integral of sqrt(2 W K). The true infimum of the interface tension cannot exceed the value from this path. Cauchy–Schwarz then gives the useful exact squared bound

sigma_min squared <= sigma_path squared <= 2 (integral_0^1 W(t) dt) (integral_0^1 K(t) dt).

One sees the first step by introducing s=dt/dz>0 in the static energy: its density integrated over t is K s/2+W/s, minimized pointwise at s=sqrt(2W/K). Endpoint singularities or interior zeros are understood by limiting finite-energy profiles. Additional paths can lower the upper bound; no lower bound follows. The argument assumes the declared degenerate global minima. Metastable minima with a deeper third branch do not by themselves define a stable cosmological wall.

For rational polynomial inputs, the code computes both integrals exactly with BigInt fractions. An explicit bias has a separate, precisely bounded algebraic question. Let O_i be specified CP-odd operators and c_i their small real coefficients. To first order, the relaxed branch-energy difference is b transpose c, where b_i=2 O_i(v), up to the chosen sign of which branch is subtracted. The derivative of a relaxed energy is the operator value at its unperturbed stationary point; the first-order vacuum shift does not contribute because the unperturbed potential gradient vanishes. The vacuum shift itself is -H inverse times the summed c_i gradient O_i.
